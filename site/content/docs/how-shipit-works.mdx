---
title: How /shipit works
description: The flow from prompt to proof. The trivial lane, playbooks, subagents, proof and the cost receipt.
---

**What it does.** `/shipit` is a router. It sizes the task, picks a process, and holds the agent to it until there is proof.

**Why.** Without a fixed process an agent skips the repro, over-builds small edits, and calls work done because the tests pass.

1. **Read the setup.** Models per role from `~/.claude/shipit/models.md`, whether berth is there, and saved rejections, which are constraints.
2. **Size the task.** A typo, config value, one-line fix or dependency bump takes the trivial lane: one change, one check, a two or three line reply. It escalates if the diff grows or the check fails twice.
3. **Pick the playbook.** A playbook is the checklist `/shipit` follows for one kind of task; you never name it. Its steps open the todo list, and a skipped step stays as `skip: <reason>`. An idea goes to Ideation and stops for your pick; a large effort goes to `/figure-it-out`.
4. **Delegate.** The lead hands bounded work to subagents, at most 5 at once, each in its own worktree and on its role's [model](/models-and-cost): Sonnet for code, Opus for judgment, Haiku for reading, a mix of families for review.
5. **Prove it.** Before done and before any PR, it builds the [proof pack](/proof).
6. **Reply with a receipt.** Outcome first, proof linked, each claim labeled measured, inferred or guess. A run with subagents ends with the total from `cost.py`.

A good prompt names the goal, what done means and the proof you want:

```text
/shipit the csv export drops its last row since yesterday's deploy. repro first, then fix. done means the 60k-row fixture exports every row. show me the row counts before and after.
```
